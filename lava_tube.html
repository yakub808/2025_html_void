LAVA TUBE HABITATION SYSTEM - situated in Lacus Mortis, a unique lunar location marked by an accessible lava tube, the Moon base is an epitome of advanced design and functionality.
The area around the cave entrance is adorned with robust infrastructure that includes two 3D-printed landing pads, equipped with storage and fuel depots,
and an intricate road network connecting to the ISRU processing refineries, Small Modular Fission Power Plants, and the lava tube entrance.
The settlement is characterized by four distinctive and self-sustaining units, each featuring a blend of a prefabricated aluminum module from Earth and a dual-layer inflatable shell.
Each unit, while functioning independently, contributes to the overall sustainability and functionality of the lunar settlement.

The settlement comprises four distinct units. Each unit blends a prefabricated aluminum module from Earth,
which houses the primary Environmental Control and Life Support System (ECLSS), with a dual-layer inflatable shell that unfolds from airlock connectors.
This design balances efficient heat radiation and structural load, and incorporates modular floors with integrated life-support systems.
Three units reside 200 meters inside the lava tube, which shields them from ionizing radiation, micrometeoroids, thermal extremes, and lunar regolith static charges.
A see-through architecture affords an exceptional view of the alien landscape.

The core module serves as the central hub, providing essential life-support systems for the different units and maintaining a stable pressurized environment.
It is constructed using a specially designed aluminum unit transported from Earth. During the setup phase, the core module inflates an outer shell to a pressure of 0.5 bar,
creating a protective barrier and a convenient area for accessing lightweight suits.
A large technical airlock allows for the transportation of modular elements into the outer shell, enabling the construction of inner modules and systems.

The outer shell, optimizes heat radiation and reduces structural loads on the inner shell, which maintains full atmospheric pressure (1 bar).
It allows to create the module for leisure, both for crew and guests, with the outside (semi-shell) greenery and terraces allow to spend time outside modules with light space suits.

Shell pressurized to a full 1 bar, housing the habitat's living and operational spaces. Within the 1-bar shell, modular floor tiles embed power, water, and air systems,
while walls are designed according to function and radiation shielding requirements. This structure combines durability and adaptability, shaping the future of space habitation.
Each of these units can function independently, yet together, they create a robust, self-sustaining lunar settlement.

Stepping through the airlock, you enter a world that seamlessly combines retro aesthetics with state-of-the-art technology.
The interior of the Moon Station reveals a captivating blend of polished metal surfaces, neon accents, and bold geometric patterns.
Vintage-inspired control panels and retro-futuristic furnishings create an atmosphere that transports you back to an era when dreams of space travel were at their zenith.
The modular installation of modules involves using pre-manufactured panels that are designed to interlock and fit together easily.
This method allows for quick and precise installation, thanks to standardized sizes and interlocking systems.
The panels integrate with other building systems and can be adapted and reused.
Modular floor panels can be easily disassembled and reinstalled, making them highly adaptable and reusable.
